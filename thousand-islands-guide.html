A Thousand Islands Boating Weekend

Castles, channels, coves and the planning details that make this border-water trip work.

Photo: remundo / Wikimedia Commons, licensed CC BY-SA 2.0.

The Thousand Islands are best approached as a cruising region, not a checklist. Pick a home base, understand the border rules and leave room for slow exploration.

Choose a practical home base

Clayton and Alexandria Bay provide convenient access to fuel, food, marinas and the central islands. Reserve dockage before busy summer weekends.

Plan one landmark and one quiet stretch

Pair a major stop such as Boldt Castle with time in quieter channels. Trying to cover the entire region in two days turns a memorable cruise into a fuel run.

Navigation and border awareness

Carry current charts, monitor weather and understand customs requirements before crossing into Canadian waters or landing. Confirm all rules with the appropriate authorities before departure.

Clayton or Alexandria Bay?

Clayton feels like a polished river town with museums, restaurants and a walkable waterfront. Alexandria Bay places the central islands and Heart Island close at hand and carries more of the classic vacation energy. Either works; the better choice depends on whether you want a town-centered weekend or the shortest route to major sights. Reserve a slip or verify launch parking before a peak summer weekend.

Boldt Castle on Heart Island

Boldt Castle is the river’s signature landmark and an easy anchor for one day of the trip. Approach with a current chart and follow the marked traffic pattern around Heart Island. Before leaving the dock, confirm current admission, operating hours and docking procedures with the official operator. Allow enough time to secure the boat and walk the grounds rather than treating the island as a quick photo stop.

A balanced weekend route

Day one: landmarks and town

Begin early enough to learn the current and traffic patterns before the busiest tour-boat window. Make one major stop, cruise a few central channels and return for an unhurried evening ashore. Use current charts continuously: islands that look comfortably separated from a distance can hide shoals, narrow passages or abrupt route choices.

Day two: quieter water

Choose a protected cove, a state-park stop or a slower island circuit that keeps the crew out of the busiest lanes. This is where the Thousand Islands become more than a postcard. Granite shorelines, small cottages and changing views around every point reward displacement-speed travel.

Navigation deserves attention

The region combines current, international waters, commercial traffic, tour boats, shallow edges and closely spaced islands. Use current charts, maintain a real lookout and slow down before uncertainty becomes urgency. A chartplotter is valuable, but zoom level can hide hazards and route lines are not permission to ignore markers.

Cross-border planning

Passing through water, landing, fishing and entering another country are not the same legal event. Requirements can change. Before departure, consult the current U.S. and Canadian border agencies and confirm what applies to every person aboard. Carry the required identification and do not rely on dockside hearsay.

The honest verdict

This is one of the Northeast’s finest cruising regions, but it rewards patience more than horsepower. Pick a modest area, learn it well and leave the full archipelago for another trip. The weekend becomes richer the moment you stop trying to count islands.
